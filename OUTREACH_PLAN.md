# Customer Outreach Plan

Interlay should sell a concrete first step instead of leading with broad custom software work. The strongest first offer is a business systems audit: a practical review of a small business's website, hosting, email, forms, manual workflows, and software stack.

## Positioning

Interlay Software helps small businesses clean up the technical systems behind daily work: websites, domains, email, hosting, forms, customer handoffs, internal tools, reporting, and practical automation.

The sharper acquisition message:

> Request a business systems audit. We will review your website, hosting, email, forms, manual workflows, and software stack, then identify the highest-impact fixes.

This lowers trust friction because prospects do not need to know whether they need a website, automation, a customer portal, hosting cleanup, or custom software. They only need to know their current setup is costing time, leads, or reliability.

## Best Customer Angles

### Website and Systems Rescue

Target businesses with outdated sites, broken forms, confusing booking flows, bad email setup, slow hosting, missing analytics, DNS problems, or no clear backup and recovery path.

Offer:

> I will audit your website, email, hosting, forms, and backups, then fix the weak spots.

### Manual Workflow Automation

Target businesses still moving work through spreadsheets, email threads, PDFs, text messages, and copy-paste.

Good fits include contractors, clinics, logistics teams, repair shops, consultants, local service businesses, niche B2B firms, and small manufacturers.

Offer:

> I will turn your recurring manual process into a simple internal tool.

### Fractional Technical Partner

Target small businesses without in-house technical leadership.

Offer a monthly retainer for site updates, hosting, email, automations, vendor coordination, backups, monitoring, and small software improvements.

### Customer Portal and Field Workflow Builds

Target businesses where customers or field teams need to submit information, track status, upload files, schedule work, approve requests, or report progress.

Offer:

> A lightweight custom portal or field workflow without buying bloated enterprise software.

## Target Niches

Pick one niche for each outreach sprint instead of contacting every kind of small business at once.

- Contractors and trade businesses
- Professional services firms
- Clinics and appointment-based local businesses
- Repair and maintenance companies
- Small manufacturers and distributors
- Logistics, delivery, and field service teams
- Niche B2B firms with repeat operational workflows

## 30-Day Acquisition Sprint

### Week 1: Pick the Market and Build the List

- Choose one niche.
- Build a list of 100 prospects.
- Prioritize businesses with visible tech or process problems.
- Record website URL, owner or manager name, email, phone, observed issue, and likely pain.
- Look for outdated websites, missing SSL, slow mobile pages, unclear contact flows, broken forms, generic email addresses, no booking flow, and obvious manual intake steps.

### Week 2: Send Specific Outreach

- Send 20 to 25 emails per day.
- Mention one concrete observed issue.
- Offer a short systems review, not a vague software project.
- Track replies, objections, booked calls, and follow-ups.

### Week 3: Follow Up and Book Audits

- Follow up with non-responders after 3 to 5 business days.
- Send a second follow-up after another week.
- Keep follow-ups useful and specific.
- Offer a fixed-scope paid audit if the prospect has clear pain but is not ready for a larger project.

### Week 4: Convert Audits Into Projects

- Deliver a simple findings summary.
- Separate fixes into urgent, high-leverage, and later improvements.
- Offer one clear next project.
- Ask every happy customer for a Google review and one referral.
- Turn completed work into a short case study, even if anonymized.

## Outreach Template

```text
Subject: Quick note on your website/forms

Hi [Name],

I run Interlay Software. I help small businesses clean up the technical stack behind their website, email, hosting, forms, and internal workflows.

I noticed [specific issue: outdated site, broken form, slow mobile page, unclear booking flow, missing contact path, generic email setup, etc.].

If useful, I can do a short systems review and point out the highest-impact fixes. No big pitch, just practical notes on what is costing time, leads, or reliability.

Nikolas
```

## Follow-Up Template

```text
Subject: Re: Quick note on your website/forms

Hi [Name],

Following up in case this is useful. The kind of review I mean is practical: website, email, hosting, forms, handoffs, backups, and any workflow that still depends on copy-paste or spreadsheets.

If there is a weak spot costing time or leads, I can identify it and suggest the simplest fix.

Nikolas
```

## Discovery Call Questions

- What does the business currently run on?
- Where do customers first submit information?
- What still happens by hand every week?
- Which workflows depend on spreadsheets, email threads, PDFs, or text messages?
- What breaks, slows down, or gets duplicated?
- Who owns the website, domain, email, hosting, backups, and analytics?
- What would save the most time if it worked better?
- What customer handoff creates the most confusion?
- What does a good outcome look like 30 days from now?

## Website Improvements To Support Outreach

- Make the business systems audit the primary call to action.
- Add problem-led copy: broken forms, spreadsheet chaos, manual approvals, customer handoffs, unreliable email, vendor confusion, and missing backups.
- Add short example project stories or anonymized case studies.
- Add industry-specific landing pages after choosing a niche.
- Use a stronger CTA than "Talk through your stack," such as "Request a systems audit."
- Keep custom software as the solution, but sell the audit as the first step.

## Recommended First Move

Start with one niche and one offer:

> Business systems audit for small businesses with outdated websites, unreliable forms, manual workflows, or unclear ownership of hosting, email, backups, and software.

This gives outreach a clear hook, gives the website a clear conversion path, and creates a natural bridge into paid fixes, custom tools, and ongoing retainers.
